A **dedicated development team** is a stable external group assigned to one product or portfolio. The model works when the product evolves continuously and the client needs durable context, not a sequence of short project handoffs.

## Design the team around the work

Start with product needs rather than a standard staffing package. A team may include product leadership, UX, frontend, backend, mobile, quality, platform, or data expertise. Not every role needs full-time allocation.

Avoid over-specialization in a small team. Too many narrow handoffs slow delivery and create queues.

## Preserve client product ownership

Name a client product owner with authority over outcomes and priorities. The vendor can provide product expertise, but cannot replace access to customers, business strategy, and organizational decisions.

Maintain one backlog and one definition of success. Separate client and vendor priority systems encourage local optimization.

## Onboard with intent

Provide business context, users, architecture, environments, standards, recent incidents, roadmap, and known risks. Start with a small real change that crosses the delivery process. Pair new members with people who understand the system.

Measure onboarding by safe contribution and context gained—not accounts created.

## Establish working agreements

Define overlap hours, planning, demonstrations, written updates, code review, testing, releases, documentation, escalation, and availability. Revisit agreements after the first month using actual friction.

## Measure the system

Useful measures include lead time, release frequency, change failure, escaped defects, recovery time, product outcomes, and team health. Avoid ranking individuals by tickets, commits, or hours. Software delivery is collaborative.

## Prevent dependency

Use client-owned repositories and accounts. Document architecture and operations as work happens. Rotate knowledge, pair across boundaries, and include client engineers where available. Plan orderly transitions in the contract.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is a dedicated team better than fixed scope?

When priorities will evolve, product knowledge compounds, and ongoing capacity is more valuable than a fixed specification.

### How long should we commit?

Allow enough time for onboarding and meaningful delivery, but use review points tied to outcomes, team health, and future need.

### Who manages the team?

The model may include vendor delivery leadership, but client product ownership and governance remain essential.
