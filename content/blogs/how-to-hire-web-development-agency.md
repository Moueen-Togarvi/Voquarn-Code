To **hire a web development agency**, define the outcome first, shortlist teams with relevant evidence, and verify how they will deliver and support the site. The best portfolio is not enough if scope, ownership, and communication remain unclear.

## What are the ten steps?

1. Define the audience, problem, and desired result.
2. List essential journeys and integrations.
3. Set a budget range and decision date.
4. Shortlist agencies with relevant complexity.
5. Review live work on mobile and desktop.
6. Meet the proposed delivery team.
7. Compare approaches, assumptions, and exclusions.
8. Check references and security practices.
9. Review ownership, payment, and change terms.
10. Start with discovery or a clear first milestone.

## What should you ask in interviews?

Ask how the agency will measure success, manage content, protect search visibility, test accessibility, handle scope changes, and respond after launch. Request an example of a difficult project decision and what evidence guided it.

The agency should also ask you substantial questions. A team that only confirms features may not be examining the business problem.

## How should portfolios be judged?

Inspect usability, speed, content clarity, responsive behavior, and business relevance. Confirm what the agency actually owned. A beautiful screenshot does not prove engineering quality or outcomes.

## What contract details matter?

Verify deliverables, schedule, client responsibilities, acceptance, change control, intellectual property, third-party licensing, confidentiality, account access, warranties, support, and termination. Seek professional advice where needed.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many agencies should I interview?

Three qualified candidates are usually enough for a meaningful comparison.

### Should I share my budget?

A realistic range helps agencies propose an appropriate approach. It does not remove the need for detailed scope and pricing.

### What is a good final test?

A paid discovery session shows the team’s questions, communication, and decision quality with limited commitment.
