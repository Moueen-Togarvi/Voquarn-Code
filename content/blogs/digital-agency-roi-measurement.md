**Digital agency ROI** should connect agency cost to incremental business value, while acknowledging that attribution is rarely perfect. The useful question is not “how many activities happened?” but “what changed, how confidently, and was it worth the investment?”

## What should be defined before work starts?

Agree on the business outcome, baseline period, target audience, conversion definitions, data sources, time horizon, and factors the agency cannot control. Verify analytics before campaigns or redesigns make the baseline harder to recover.

## Which metrics matter?

Use a measurement chain:

- Reach among the relevant audience.
- Meaningful engagement or task completion.
- Qualified leads or transactions.
- Sales acceptance and close rate.
- Revenue, margin, or customer value.
- Delivery and media cost.
- Incremental profit where measurable.

Leading indicators help teams act sooner, but they should connect logically to a commercial or operational outcome.

## How should ROI be calculated?

A simple expression is: incremental value minus total investment, divided by total investment. Include agency fees, media, software, production, and material internal cost. Use gross profit rather than revenue when margin varies significantly.

## What are attribution limits?

Customers may encounter search, social, referrals, email, and sales conversations before buying. Platform-reported attribution can over-credit its own channel. Use consistent analytics, CRM evidence, controlled tests where possible, and explicit confidence levels.

## Frequently asked questions

### How soon should ROI appear?

It depends on the channel and buying cycle. Paid campaigns can generate signals quickly; SEO, brand, and complex B2B pipelines often take longer.

### Are impressions a useful metric?

They show exposure, not business value. Use them as a diagnostic metric within a larger chain.

### What should an agency report monthly?

Results against baseline, insight, completed work, experiments, risks, next actions, and data limitations.
