Before signing with a **Custom software development company**, examine how the work will be delivered after the sales process ends. Delivery due diligence tests whether proposed governance, engineering, and ownership can survive real uncertainty.

## Inspect the path from request to acceptance

Ask the company to walk through discovery, prioritization, design, implementation, review, testing, deployment, and acceptance using a previous project. Identify who made decisions and what evidence moved work forward.

## Review reporting artifacts

Request anonymized examples of a weekly update, risk register, release note, architecture decision, and acceptance record. Useful reporting shows outcomes, budget, uncertainty, decisions, and quality—not only completed tickets.

## Test change governance

Present a realistic change: a required integration behaves differently than expected. Ask how the team investigates, communicates options, estimates impact, and obtains a decision. Fixed scope does not eliminate change; it changes how commercial risk is handled.

## Verify technical stewardship

Discuss code review, automated checks, dependency management, environments, deployment, monitoring, backup, incident response, and documentation. Confirm how technical debt is made visible and prioritized.

## Check launch and transition

The plan should cover migration, user preparation, analytics, support, alerting, rollback, warranty, and enhancement ownership. Verify that repositories, cloud accounts, and third-party services remain accessible if the relationship ends.

## Evaluate continuity risk

Ask which parts of delivery depend on one person, proprietary internal tools, or supplier-owned accounts. Require role backup, shared reviews, documentation, and client access throughout the engagement. A continuity plan should cover staff turnover, vendor interruption, and orderly exit without waiting for a crisis to reveal missing knowledge.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can due diligence be done before discovery?

Yes. Evaluate process and evidence before award, then use paid discovery to test the actual working relationship.

### What is the most useful reference question?

Ask a former client how the company behaved when scope, quality, or timing became difficult.

### Who should participate in selection?

Include product, technical, operational, security, commercial, and executive owners according to project risk.
