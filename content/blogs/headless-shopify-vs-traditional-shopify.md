Use **traditional Shopify** when its theme architecture can deliver the required storefront efficiently. Consider **headless Shopify** when experience, content, channel, or integration requirements clearly outweigh the added development and operating complexity.

## What does headless Shopify mean?

Shopify remains the commerce backend while a separate frontend retrieves commerce data through APIs. This gives developers greater control over presentation and can support multiple customer touchpoints.

Traditional Shopify uses the platform’s integrated storefront and theme system, keeping editing, apps, and operations more closely connected.

## What benefits can headless provide?

Potential benefits include a highly tailored frontend, integration with a separate content platform, shared content across channels, and independent frontend release workflows. These benefits depend on strong implementation; headless does not guarantee speed or conversion.

## What complexity does it add?

Plan for custom hosting, API behavior, caching, preview, search, analytics, app compatibility, account flows, error monitoring, deployment, and developer support. Some theme-oriented apps will require custom integration.

## When should you avoid headless?

Avoid it when the main reason is trend appeal, the standard theme can meet requirements, the team lacks long-term engineering capacity, or the commercial gain is unclear. A well-built traditional storefront can be the more adaptable business choice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does headless Shopify replace Shopify checkout?

Usually the storefront is separate while checkout continues through supported Shopify flows. Validate exact requirements and current platform capabilities.

### Is headless always faster?

No. Performance depends on frontend code, data fetching, caching, media, third-party scripts, and infrastructure.

### How should we decide?

Prototype the critical journey, estimate total ownership cost, and document which limitations headless resolves.
