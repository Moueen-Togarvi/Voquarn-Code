A **SaaS development company** should understand recurring customer value and recurring operational responsibility. Subscription software needs a product loop, tenant controls, billing, service ownership, and continuous learning.

## Evaluate market and product discovery

Ask how the company identifies target users, painful jobs, alternatives, willingness to pay, and activation. The team should reduce an MVP to one complete value journey rather than one screen from many features.

## Inspect SaaS foundations

Review tenant isolation, identity, roles, invitations, data export, audit, integrations, configuration, backups, and support administration. Avoid overengineering scale while protecting boundaries that are expensive to retrofit.

## Review subscription expertise

Billing includes plans, trials, usage, entitlements, payment failure, taxes, invoices, upgrades, cancellation, and refunds. Ask how provider events remain synchronized with product access.

## Design for retention

Onboarding, empty states, templates, imports, education, notifications, and customer support help users reach value. Analytics should measure activation and repeated core behavior by cohort.

## Verify product operations

Confirm monitoring, incident response, tenant diagnostics, feature rollout, data recovery, privacy requests, customer communication, and maintenance. SaaS is operated continuously.

## Inspect customer learning practice

Ask how the company combines analytics, interviews, support issues, cancellations, and sales feedback without allowing the loudest customer to control the roadmap. Product reviews should connect evidence with hypotheses and opportunity cost. Delivery speed is useful only when the team learns what deserves to be delivered.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should the company help with product strategy?

Yes, while founders retain market and product accountability. Technical delivery should be informed by customer evidence.

### Which architecture should SaaS use?

Choose the simplest design that safely supports tenants, expected scale, team ownership, and change. Microservices are not a requirement.

### What should the contract address?

Scope, team, IP, data, accounts, security, support, third-party costs, termination, and transition.
