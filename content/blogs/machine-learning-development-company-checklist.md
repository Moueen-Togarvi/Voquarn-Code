A **machine learning development company** should prove that a predictive system outperforms a practical baseline and can operate safely after deployment. Model accuracy alone does not establish business value.

## Problem and decision framing

Define the decision the prediction informs, who acts on it, timing, error costs, and current baseline. A statistically strong model can be useless if its output arrives too late or nobody trusts it.

## Data assessment

Review availability, lineage, representativeness, labels, leakage, missing values, bias, permissions, and change over time. Ask how the company separates training, validation, and test data and prevents future information from leaking into historical features.

## Experiment discipline

Require simple baselines, documented experiments, appropriate metrics, reproducibility, and error analysis by meaningful segments. The best metric depends on business cost; average accuracy may hide harmful failures.

## Production engineering

Inspect feature pipelines, model registry, deployment, versioning, rollback, batch or online serving, observability, and integration. Define how code, data, and model versions reproduce a decision.

## Monitoring and governance

Track input quality, drift, prediction distribution, latency, failures, business outcomes, and fairness where relevant. Establish thresholds, review cadence, retraining approval, and incident response.

## Hiring questions

- What baseline will you compare against?
- How will you detect leakage and bias?
- Which errors matter most to the business?
- How will a prediction be explained and challenged?
- Who approves model changes?
- What assets and accounts will we own?

## Frequently asked questions

### How much data is enough?

It depends on problem complexity, signal, labels, variation, and required confidence. Begin with a feasibility assessment rather than a universal number.

### Do we need deep learning?

Not necessarily. Simpler models can be more economical, explainable, and reliable.

### When should ML not be used?

When rules solve the task, data is insufficient, errors are unacceptable without human review, or no workflow will use the output.
