Hiring an **answer engine optimization agency** has become a common response to falling click-through rates. The category is young, so credentials are hard to read and the gap between the best and worst providers is unusually wide.

This guide sets out what the engagement should include, how to test a provider's understanding, and which promises indicate that a firm has renamed its old service without changing its method.

## What answer engine optimization covers

Answer engine optimization targets systems that respond with a written answer: assistants, AI overviews inside conventional search, and question-answering features inside platforms. The goal is being selected as a source and described accurately.

The work divides into three streams. Technical work makes content reachable and parseable by AI crawlers. Content work makes individual claims quotable and self-contained. Authority work makes those claims corroborated by sources outside your control.

An agency strong in only one stream will plateau. Perfect technical access does not help if no independent source confirms what you say, and excellent PR does not help if the crawler cannot render your pages.

## Questions that separate qualified providers

Ask these in the first call and listen for specifics.

**"How will you measure where we stand today?"** A qualified answer describes a prompt set, repeated sampling across assistants and phrasings, and a baseline citation rate. A weak answer talks about keyword rankings.

**"Which crawlers do you check for, and how?"** They should name the specific AI crawler user agents, describe how they verify access at the CDN and robots level, and mention rendering. If the answer is generic "technical search optimization", press further.

**"What happens when we are cited inaccurately?"** Misdescription is a real and common outcome. A provider who has done this work has a correction playbook: fix the source claim, strengthen corroboration, address contradictory third-party content.

**"What are the limits of your reporting?"** Honest providers volunteer that assistant outputs vary, that referral attribution is incomplete, and that their tracking is sampled rather than exhaustive. A provider claiming precise, complete measurement is overselling.

## Deliverables to write into the contract

- A documented prompt set, owned by you, covering the buying questions in your category.
- Baseline and recurring citation measurement with the method and sample size stated.
- A crawler access and rendering audit with specific remediation items.
- Structured data implementation covering organization, product or service, and question-answer content.
- A content restructuring plan for existing pages, not only a new-article calendar.
- A corroboration plan naming target publications, directories, and reference sources.
- Monthly reporting that separates what changed on your side from what changed in the models.

## Pricing models and what they suit

Retainers suit ongoing programs where content and authority work continue month over month, and they are the common structure. Project pricing suits a defined audit and remediation phase, which is a sensible way to start a relationship before committing to a retainer.

Performance pricing tied to citations is uncommon and worth scrutinizing, because the metric is sampled and the provider usually controls the measurement. If you use it, insist that measurement runs on a method you can reproduce.

## Warning signs

Treat these as reasons to keep looking: guaranteed placement in a named assistant; a claim to have a "direct relationship" that influences model output; refusal to disclose the tracking method; a proposal identical to a standard search-optimization retainer with terminology substituted; and pricing built around volume of articles rather than claims owned.

Also be cautious of providers who advise abandoning conventional search optimization. The authority signals that generative engines depend on are largely produced by that work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is AEO different from GEO?

The terms overlap heavily in practice. Answer engine optimization usually emphasizes direct question answering; generative engine optimization is often used more broadly. Judge providers on method rather than vocabulary.

### Can we do this in-house?

Partly. Technical fixes and content restructuring are within reach of a capable internal team. Corroboration across third-party sources is where most in-house programs stall for lack of relationships.

### How large should the prompt set be?

Enough to cover your main buying questions with several phrasings each. A few dozen well-chosen prompts measured consistently beats hundreds measured once.

### How quickly should we expect movement?

Access and rendering fixes can register within weeks. Citation share for competitive commercial questions typically moves over one to two quarters.
