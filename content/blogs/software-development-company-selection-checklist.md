**Select a software development company by comparing evidence from the people who will deliver your project.** Ask each shortlisted team to explain the same difficult workflow, inspect how it verifies a release, and check a relevant client reference. A portfolio can establish visual quality; it cannot establish who maintains production or what happens when requirements change.

This guide focuses on the evaluation meeting and final selection decision. For the earlier work of preparing scope, ownership and commercial inputs for a Pakistan-based engagement, use our [Pakistan software procurement checklist](/blog/custom-software-development-pakistan-procurement-checklist-2026).

## Give every candidate the same evaluation case

First establish why custom work is needed. A distinctive workflow or a missing integration may justify development; an unclear problem may call for research or a suitable existing product. Ask candidates to explain that choice before assessing their ability to deliver a predetermined solution.

Prepare a short, anonymized scenario instead of sending a different brief to every company. For example, a distributor needs orders approved by branch managers before stock is reserved. An approved order can subsequently be cancelled, and the accounting system sometimes responds late. Ask candidates what they would investigate before estimating delivery.

A useful response should distinguish confirmed requirements from assumptions. Does cancellation release reserved stock? Can a manager approve their own order? What should users see while accounting is unavailable? These questions reveal reasoning about the business, not merely familiarity with a framework.

Give suppliers enough context to respond fairly. Do not expect a complete design or unpaid implementation during a sales meeting. Where substantial investigation is required, agree a bounded, paid discovery exercise.

## Use an evidence scale with clear definitions

Assign weights before presentations so a persuasive salesperson cannot quietly change your priorities. An illustrative scorecard might give delivery and recovery evidence 30 points, problem understanding 25, security 20, team continuity 15, and commercial clarity 10. These are example weights, not industry benchmarks; a regulated product may need a different balance.

For each criterion, record a simple evidence level:

- **0 — Unknown:** no relevant answer or artifact was supplied.
- **1 — Claimed:** the company describes a process without showing it.
- **2 — Demonstrated:** a relevant, redacted example supports the claim.
- **3 — Verified:** the proposed team demonstrates it and a reference or exercise confirms it.

Calculate a weighted contribution as the criterion weight multiplied by the evidence level divided by three. Keep the supporting notes beside the score. A high total should never override a blocking issue such as unclear source-code rights or refusal to provide client access to production accounts.

## Inspect one change from request to release

Ask a candidate to walk through a completed change on a project it is allowed to discuss. Follow the original request, the decision made, review comments, verification and deployment. The purpose is to understand the chain of responsibility, not to collect confidential client code.

Look for evidence that requirements changed in response to learning. A release history with only polished screenshots tells you little about whether the team can resolve conflicting priorities. A redacted defect report and the regression check added afterward can be more informative.

For security, [NIST's Secure Software Development Framework](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/final) provides a shared vocabulary for discussing supplier practices. Use it to ask how security requirements, protected development environments and vulnerability response fit into delivery. Referencing a framework alone does not prove implementation or certification.

## Interview the proposed team, not just the company

Meet the delivery lead and an engineer who would actually work on the engagement. Ask them to explain the example independently, then discuss it together. You are checking whether responsibilities and understanding are shared.

Confirm who makes architectural decisions, who reviews releases, and who covers absence. For a Pakistan-based team serving international clients, establish a practical overlap window and an escalation route outside it. Do not confuse round-the-clock sales availability with funded production support.

Ask these concrete questions:

- Which named person can approve a release to production?
- How much of each person's time is committed to other projects?
- Who can restore service if the lead engineer is unavailable?
- What happens when an assigned person leaves or is replaced?
- Which decisions require the client's approval before work continues?
- What information will the client receive between demonstrations?

## Check references for difficult moments

With permission, speak to a client whose project resembles your operating risk. A marketing site reference does not establish competence with financial reconciliation, even if both products use the same language.

Ask what the supplier owned, what the client still had to provide, and how actual delivery differed from the proposal. Explore an incident or disputed requirement. Did the team explain the evidence, accept responsibility where appropriate, and leave a usable record? A reference that says everything was perfect may simply lack enough detail for your decision.

Verify the relationship through a channel you trust. Treat references as one input; they are usually selected by the supplier and may not describe every engagement.

## Run a small paid exercise when uncertainty matters

Choose a vertical slice that exposes an important risk. For the distributor example, this could be submitting an order, rejecting a duplicate request, and recording an approval with the correct permissions. Use synthetic data and a test environment.

Agree the exercise's ownership, budget limit and deliverables before starting. Require a reproducible setup, a demonstration of a failure case, and a short explanation of unresolved assumptions. Avoid assessing suppliers by how many screens they complete in a weekend.

If accessibility matters to your users, specify a relevant [WCAG standard](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) and ask for keyboard and assistive-technology evidence for the chosen journey. A general promise to make the application accessible is difficult to evaluate; a demonstrated task is concrete.

For a website agency, include a real content page, a mobile enquiry journey and an editor publishing a correction. Check that analytics captures the intended action without duplicating it, and ask for measured loading behavior on representative devices. These observations connect design, content, accessibility and engineering evidence to the same selection process.

## Make the final decision reviewable

Compare the same evidence level across candidates. If one supplier offers a lower price because migration and support are excluded, adjust the scope comparison before treating the quote as better value. Record unresolved issues and the evidence required to close them.

The resulting decision should fit on a page: preferred team, reasons, material assumptions, rejected alternatives and conditions before work begins. Carry those conditions into the [software development contract checklist](/blog/software-development-contract-checklist), rather than leaving them in a sales email.

## Questions buyers commonly ask

### Should we choose the company with the most years of experience?

Company age is context. Recent, relevant evidence from the assigned people is more useful than a long history in an unrelated product category. Ask how experienced staff will remain involved after the contract begins.

### Is a paid trial always necessary?

No. A small, well-understood project may have enough evidence already. A trial helps when an integration, handover or operating requirement could materially change the decision and cannot be resolved through references and existing artifacts.
