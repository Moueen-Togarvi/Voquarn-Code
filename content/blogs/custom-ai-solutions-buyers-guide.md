**Custom AI solutions** are justified when proprietary workflows, data, integrations, controls, or user experience create value that a packaged assistant cannot support. Custom does not necessarily mean training a model; it often means engineering the system around one.

## Run a fit-gap assessment

Compare packaged tools against required users, data sources, permissions, actions, quality, integration, branding, regions, and economics. Classify gaps as configurable, integrable, acceptable workarounds, or genuinely differentiating.

Build only where the gap has measurable consequence.

## Define the custom value

Examples include domain-specific retrieval, proprietary decision support, access-aware knowledge, specialized document processing, controlled system actions, or AI embedded in an existing product journey.

Write acceptance criteria and baseline before selecting models.

## Own the important assets

Clarify ownership of source, prompts, evaluation sets, data preparation, indexes, tool definitions, telemetry, interfaces, and accounts. Model weights may remain owned by a provider, but your differentiating workflow should be portable where practical.

## Evaluate and control behavior

Use representative scenarios, expert review, permissions, validation, human approval, audit trails, and operational monitoring. Custom systems can fail in custom ways, so generic benchmarks are not sufficient.

## Model total cost

Include discovery, integration, data maintenance, inference, monitoring, expert review, provider changes, support, and improvement. Compare unit cost per successful task rather than price per token alone.

## Design for organizational adoption

Involve the people whose work will change in prototypes and pilots. Define training, feedback, support, and responsibility for disputed output. Measure whether the solution improves the complete workflow, not only model behavior. A technically accurate custom system can still fail when it adds review effort or conflicts with existing incentives.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do custom solutions require model training?

No. Retrieval, tools, workflow, evaluation, and interface customization often create the value.

### When should we buy instead?

Buy when the workflow is standard, packaged capability fits, and differentiation does not justify product ownership.

### What should a pilot prove?

Task value, quality under representative conditions, integration feasibility, risk controls, adoption, and economics.
