**Evaluate an AI workflow automation company by the evidence it produces for your actual process, including exceptions and ongoing operation.** Give candidates the same bounded task, source examples and acceptance conditions. A proposal listing many agents or models is less useful than a demonstrated workflow that your team can inspect, maintain and stop when necessary.

Suppose a distributor wants help processing emailed purchase requests. The desired outcome is a correct draft order with missing information routed to staff. The project includes mailbox access, document parsing, product matching, approval and an ERP connector. A demo that summarizes an email addresses only a small part of that delivery.

## Write a brief that separates the problem from the proposed tool

Describe the current process, frequency, delays and common corrections. Identify the users and the person accountable for accepting the result. Include representative difficult cases instead of only a polished example.

State which outcomes matter: fewer transcription corrections, shorter time to a reviewable draft or better handling of incomplete requests. Do not begin by requiring a multi-agent architecture unless there is a specific reason it is necessary.

Ask candidates what should remain deterministic. Product identifiers, approval limits and order totals may be better handled through ordinary code and existing systems. A competent provider should explain where language interpretation adds value and where it would add avoidable uncertainty.

## Use a shared evaluation exercise

Give shortlisted teams the same sanitized sample set and integration constraints. Ask them to identify missing information, propose a narrow implementation and demonstrate the most uncertain boundary.

For the distributor, that boundary might be matching an informal item description to the correct catalog record while rejecting an ambiguous match. The pilot should show both a correct draft and a case that stops for review.

Agree on deliverables before the exercise: a working slice, assumptions, observed errors, operating requirements and a revised scope. The purpose is to reduce uncertainty, not to obtain a free finished system or reward the most theatrical presentation.

## Ask for acceptance evidence in plain language

Define observable conditions that the business can assess. A useful acceptance set might require:

- A permitted user can submit a representative request.
- The correct catalog item is selected or ambiguity is exposed.
- Totals are calculated by validated application logic.
- The draft remains separate from an approved order.
- A missing field enters the assigned review queue.
- A connector timeout does not create a duplicate record.
- An operator can trace the result to its source and release version.
- A new team member can run the documented recovery procedure.

Evaluation tooling can assist with language outputs. The [Google Cloud evaluation overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/evaluation-overview) is one example of available approaches. Ask the provider how automated assessment is calibrated against human judgment and authoritative system state.

## Examine the integration work hidden behind the demo

Request a data-flow diagram showing identity, source systems, model providers, tools and storage. Determine where permissions are enforced and which data leaves the organization's environment.

Ask the team to demonstrate an expired credential, a changed source record and a failed write. Their recovery design reveals whether they understand production operation or have only connected a happy-path sequence.

Review the [tool calling reliability guide](/blog/tool-calling-reliability) when discussing retries and side effects. For source-heavy workflows, the [data readiness checklist](/blog/ai-data-readiness-implementation-checklist-2026) helps identify work that can otherwise emerge late as an unexpected extra.

## Compare the people who will do the work

Meet the proposed technical lead and the people responsible for integration and testing. Ask how they divide responsibility with your internal process owner. A case study delivered by an unrelated team is limited evidence for this engagement.

For Pakistan-based delivery serving international clients, agree on practical communication overlap, escalation contacts and access ownership. Location alone does not establish quality or savings. Compare the ability to work with your systems and decision process.

Ask for a sanitized example of a difficult delivery issue: an integration that failed, a model change that regressed quality or a requirement that was removed after testing. Look for evidence of diagnosis and correction rather than a claim that projects never encounter problems.

## Normalize one-time and recurring costs

Separate discovery, source preparation, integration, interface work, testing, deployment and training. Then identify recurring model usage, hosting, monitoring, human review, maintenance and support responsibilities.

Request workload assumptions for normal and busy periods. Include retries, long documents and exception handling. A low model price does not imply a low cost per completed business task if every result requires extensive repair.

Use the [AI automation budgeting guide](/blog/ai-automation-cost-pakistan-workflow-budget-2026) to structure scope questions. Avoid treating an illustrative estimate as a fixed market price or a promise of savings for a different organization.

## Put change and risk ownership into the operating plan

Name who approves prompt changes, tool permissions, model upgrades and new source integrations. Decide who monitors incidents after launch and what support response the engagement actually includes.

The [NIST AI RMF Playbook](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/nist-ai-rmf-playbook) offers voluntary practices for managing AI risk. A provider can use such guidance to structure evidence, but citing a framework does not substitute for showing your system's controls.

Require access to the repository, deployment configuration, evaluation cases and decision records under the agreed ownership arrangements. Keep production accounts recoverable by the organization. Handover should include a practical operation exercise, not only a folder of documents delivered on the last day.

## Make expansion conditional on observed results

Review the first release with the people doing the work. Compare completed outcomes, correction effort, unresolved cases and operating cost against the baseline. Record where the system helps and where it still creates work.

Expand the workflow only when the next step has a clear purpose and adequate evidence. Adding more departments, languages and action authority at once makes it difficult to understand new failures or reverse a problematic release.

### Should the initial proposal contain a fixed total price?

A fixed price is most interpretable when scope, dependencies and acceptance are sufficiently defined. For unresolved integration or data questions, a bounded discovery phase can produce the evidence needed for a meaningful delivery estimate.

### What distinguishes a strong provider from a polished sales pitch?

The team explains uncertainty, demonstrates the difficult path, makes assumptions testable and leaves artifacts your organization can use. Judge those behaviors alongside the proposed people, commercial terms and support model.
