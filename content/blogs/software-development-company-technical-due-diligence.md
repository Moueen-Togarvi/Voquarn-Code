Technical due diligence on a **Software development company** verifies that marketing claims are supported by people, practices, and evidence. The depth should match the consequence of the product.

## Meet the proposed engineers

Interview the product lead, technical lead, and specialists assigned to the engagement. Discuss a relevant system, architecture tradeoff, production failure, and lesson. Confirm allocation and replacement terms.

## Review engineering evidence

Where confidentiality permits, inspect a sample repository or anonymized artifacts for structure, tests, reviews, documentation, dependency management, and build automation. A short technical exercise should resemble real work rather than algorithm trivia.

## Examine security practice

Ask how the company controls staff access, devices, secrets, repositories, pipelines, environments, subcontractors, and production. Review secure-development training, vulnerability handling, and incident notification.

## Inspect delivery performance

Request examples of planning, demonstrations, acceptance, risk reporting, release frequency, escaped defects, and recovery. Speak with references about difficult periods.

## Verify operational maturity

Discuss monitoring, logs, alerts, service objectives, backup restoration, on-call, incidents, capacity, and maintenance. A company that only builds and hands off may not fit a critical product.

## Protect ownership

Confirm source, infrastructure, data, documentation, accounts, designs, and licenses. Ensure access is continuous rather than promised at final payment.

## Review commercial resilience

Inspect financial and staffing continuity proportionate to project importance. Clarify professional insurance where relevant, subcontractor reliance, key-person risk, business continuity, and exit assistance. Technical quality cannot protect a client from a supplier relationship that becomes unavailable without usable handover.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should every buyer review code?

Use a qualified reviewer for significant products. For smaller work, process evidence and a paid discovery phase may be proportionate.

### Are certifications enough?

No. They support knowledge claims but do not prove applied judgment or delivery fit.

### What is a strong pilot?

A bounded vertical feature that tests discovery, code, quality, deployment, and communication.
