A **web development agency for a website redesign** should improve measurable business and user outcomes while protecting what already works. A redesign is not merely a new visual theme; it is a controlled change to content, journeys, technology, and search signals.

## What should happen before design?

Create a baseline using analytics, search data, customer questions, sales feedback, page inventory, technical performance, and accessibility findings. Identify which pages attract qualified visitors and which journeys generate leads or sales.

Without a baseline, a new site can look better while performing worse.

## How should an agency protect SEO?

The redesign plan should map old URLs to new destinations, retain valuable content, preserve titles and structured information where appropriate, manage canonical rules, and test redirects before launch. Crawl both versions and monitor indexing and traffic afterward.

## Which technical risks need attention?

Review forms, CRM connections, analytics events, consent tools, payment flows, search, downloads, and third-party scripts. Confirm performance budgets, browser coverage, error monitoring, backups, and rollback steps.

## How should redesign success be measured?

Use metrics tied to the original problem: qualified inquiries, ecommerce conversion, task completion, content engagement, organic landing-page performance, page speed, or reduced support demand. Compare results across equivalent time periods and account for campaign changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should all old content be migrated?

No. Keep, improve, merge, redirect, or retire content based on value and user need. Do not delete pages solely because the design changed.

### Can a redesign launch all at once?

Yes, but complex sites may benefit from staged releases or feature flags to limit risk.

### How long should results be monitored?

Monitor technical health immediately and business performance across several meaningful traffic cycles.
