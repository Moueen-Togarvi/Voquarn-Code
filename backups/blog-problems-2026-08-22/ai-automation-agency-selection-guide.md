An **AI automation agency** should improve a business process, not maximize the number of automated steps. Buyers should select on operational understanding, integration quality, controls, and measurable results.

## Test process discovery

Ask the agency to map one workflow from trigger to outcome, including people, systems, decisions, data, exceptions, and baseline. It should identify waste and ambiguity before recommending AI.

## Review technical depth

Many automations connect CRM, email, documents, databases, messaging, payments, or internal tools. The team should understand APIs, authentication, retries, idempotency, reconciliation, secrets, and monitoring—not only visual workflow builders.

## Inspect AI controls

Ask how outputs are evaluated, permissions enforced, sensitive data protected, and humans involved. Consequential actions need validation and approval. The proposal should include failure handling and operating cost.

## Demand outcome measurement

Agree on cycle time, human effort, accuracy, backlog, conversion, resolution, or cost per task. Measure before and after under comparable conditions. The agency should report exceptions and unintended effects.

## Confirm ownership

Use client-controlled workflow, model, cloud, and integration accounts where practical. Require documentation, credentials handover, monitoring, and a transition plan.

## Review maintenance before signing

Workflow vendors, APIs, model behavior, business rules, and staff responsibilities will change. Require a maintenance plan covering connector failures, credential rotation, evaluation updates, cost alerts, workflow versions, and incident response. Clarify which changes are included in support and which become new project work. This prevents a useful pilot from becoming an unsupported operational dependency.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should an agency guarantee time savings?

It can estimate and measure them, but should not guarantee outcomes before observing workflow, adoption, and data quality.

### No-code or custom development?

Use no-code for bounded, supported workflows. Use custom code when reliability, logic, volume, security, or product experience requires it.

### What is a good first project?

A frequent, low-to-moderate-risk workflow with accessible data, clear ownership, and measurable manual effort.
