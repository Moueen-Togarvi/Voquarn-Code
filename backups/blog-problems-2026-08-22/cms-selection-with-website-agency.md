A **website agency should choose a CMS** from your publishing workflow, content structure, integrations, governance, and maintenance capacity. The best CMS is the one your organization can operate safely and efficiently—not the one the agency happens to use most.

## What questions should be answered first?

Clarify:

- Who creates, reviews, translates, and publishes content?
- Which content types and relationships exist?
- Are page layouts controlled or highly flexible?
- Which CRM, commerce, search, or personalization tools connect?
- What permissions and audit history are required?
- Who maintains updates, backups, and security?
- Must content be shared across websites or apps?
- How important are data portability and vendor independence?

## Traditional or headless CMS?

A traditional CMS manages content and presentation together, often simplifying common marketing sites. A headless CMS exposes content through APIs, which helps when several frontends share structured content or the presentation layer needs more control.

Headless architecture also adds development, preview, deployment, and integration complexity. It should solve a real need.

## How should platforms be compared?

Ask the agency for a documented scorecard covering editor experience, technical fit, security, performance, ecosystem, accessibility, hosting, support, licensing, migration, and exit options. Test the editorial workflow with representative content before committing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should editors be able to change every layout?

Usually not. Controlled flexibility protects accessibility, performance, and brand consistency while still supporting real publishing needs.

### Is open source always cheaper?

No. Licensing may be lower, but hosting, updates, security, implementation, and specialist support still cost money.

### Can the CMS be changed later?

Yes, but migration can be substantial. Structured content and clean exports reduce lock-in.
