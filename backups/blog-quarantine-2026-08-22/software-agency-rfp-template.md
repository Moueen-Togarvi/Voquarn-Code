A useful **software agency RFP** explains the business problem, decision process, and known constraints while leaving room for expert recommendations. It should help qualified agencies propose a delivery approach—not force them to guess from a long feature list.

## What should the RFP include?

Start with the following sections:

1. Organization and product context.
2. Problem, users, and desired business outcome.
3. Current process or system.
4. Required capabilities and optional ideas.
5. Known integrations, data, security, and compliance needs.
6. Target dates and reasons behind them.
7. Available budget range.
8. Expected deliverables and support.
9. Proposal format, questions, and evaluation criteria.
10. Procurement schedule and decision owners.

Separate confirmed requirements from assumptions. If you already have research, analytics, designs, or technical documentation, say what selected vendors can review.

## What should you ask the agency to provide?

Request the proposed team, delivery phases, responsibilities, assumptions, exclusions, commercial model, risk approach, and two relevant examples. Ask who will own code, design files, infrastructure accounts, and third-party licenses.

Invite vendors to challenge the scope. The strongest response may recommend a smaller first release or an investigation before committing to the full build.

## How should proposals be scored?

Use a consistent scorecard covering problem understanding, relevant evidence, technical approach, team quality, communication, risk, cost clarity, and support. Weight the criteria before reading proposals so a polished sales presentation does not distort the decision.

## What should you avoid?

Do not require speculative fixed prices for undefined work, demand a full unpaid solution design, or hide the budget solely to test vendors. Avoid hundreds of mandatory questions that do not affect selection.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should an RFP be?

Long enough to support an informed response, but short enough to keep the problem visible. Clear attachments are better than repeating background throughout the document.

### Should agencies receive a question period?

Yes. Shared written answers keep the process fair and expose important gaps early.

### Is a paid discovery better than a detailed RFP?

For uncertain products, often yes. Use the RFP to select a discovery partner, then estimate delivery from evidence.
