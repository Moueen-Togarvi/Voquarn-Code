Use a **web agency project** for a defined launch or redesign. Use a **web agency retainer** for a continuous backlog of optimization, content, maintenance, and experiments. Many organizations need a project first and a smaller retainer afterward.

## How does a project work?

A project has a target outcome, planned scope, milestones, and completion criteria. It creates focus and makes procurement easier. Its main limitation is that new learning or changing priorities require explicit change control.

## How does a retainer work?

A retainer reserves agency capacity or defined recurring services each month. It fits websites that support active marketing, ecommerce, recruitment, or frequent publishing. The team prioritizes a changing queue instead of committing to one final scope.

## What should be measured?

For projects, measure whether the agreed outcome, quality, timing, and handover are achieved. For retainers, track delivered improvements and business or service metrics such as qualified conversions, release frequency, incident response, or organic growth.

## What terms need clarity?

Confirm included skills, capacity, rollover, turnaround, urgent requests, meeting time, reporting, third-party expenses, termination, and ownership. A retainer should not imply unlimited work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a retainer include a redesign?

A gradual redesign can fit, but a dedicated project may provide clearer scope and momentum for a large change.

### Is a retainer cheaper?

Not necessarily. It buys continuity and flexible access. Value depends on whether the organization maintains a useful, prioritized backlog.

### What if we do not use all capacity?

Agree in advance whether time rolls over, expires, or is redirected to preventive work.
