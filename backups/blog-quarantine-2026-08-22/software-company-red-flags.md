The clearest **software company red flags** are promises without evidence, unclear ownership, and a delivery team hidden behind sales language. One concern may be explainable; several unresolved concerns usually signal avoidable project risk.

## Which warning signs matter most?

Check for these twelve issues:

1. A guaranteed price and deadline before the scope is understood.
2. No access to the people who will do the work.
3. Case studies that do not explain the company’s actual role.
4. No written assumptions or exclusions.
5. Progress measured only by hours or ticket counts.
6. Source code, domains, or cloud accounts controlled solely by the vendor.
7. Testing and security described as final-stage activities.
8. No approach to backups, monitoring, or incident response.
9. Vague change-request pricing.
10. No handover or documentation plan.
11. Pressure to choose a technology without discussing constraints.
12. No references willing to discuss difficult project moments.

## How can you verify claims?

Ask for a walkthrough of one relevant project, an anonymized status report, sample documentation, and an explanation of a real tradeoff. Meet the proposed delivery lead and a senior engineer. References should be asked about communication, surprises, and support—not just satisfaction.

## What should a trustworthy proposal contain?

Look for the problem statement, scope, deliverables, team, responsibilities, schedule, dependencies, assumptions, exclusions, acceptance method, commercial terms, ownership, and support. Unknowns should be acknowledged and assigned a way to resolve them.

## What if the company has a small portfolio?

A newer company can still be capable. Evaluate the team’s individual evidence, paid discovery work, technical reasoning, and willingness to structure a low-risk first phase.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a low estimate always a red flag?

No. It becomes concerning when important work is absent or the assumptions cannot be explained.

### Should a software company promise zero bugs?

No serious team can guarantee that. It should explain prevention, detection, prioritization, and response.

### What is the best risk-reduction step?

Start with a bounded paid phase that produces useful artifacts and demonstrates how the team works.
