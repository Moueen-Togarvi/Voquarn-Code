Migrate a **custom ecommerce website to Shopify** when platform capabilities cover the important workflows and reducing technical ownership is worth the constraints. Do not migrate until catalog, checkout, integrations, SEO, and total cost have been tested against real requirements.

## What problems can migration solve?

A move may reduce infrastructure maintenance, simplify routine store operations, provide a larger app ecosystem, and make standard commerce features easier to manage. It will not fix poor product data, unclear merchandising, weak fulfillment, or an untested offer by itself.

## What should be assessed first?

Document:

- Products, variants, collections, pricing, and discounts.
- Customers, orders, subscriptions, and loyalty data.
- Payments, tax, shipping, returns, and refunds.
- ERP, CRM, PIM, marketplace, and support integrations.
- Customer accounts and authentication.
- Current URLs, organic landing pages, and structured data.
- Analytics events and marketing feeds.
- Custom workflows that create business value.

Classify each requirement as native, app-supported, custom extension, process change, or unsupported.

## How should migration be executed?

Clean and map data, build the storefront, integrate systems, rehearse imports, test complete journeys, prepare redirects, and define a cutover and rollback plan. Freeze or reconcile changing data during the final move so orders and stock are not lost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will rankings fall after migration?

Temporary change is possible. Preserve valuable content, map URLs carefully, test redirects, and monitor crawl, index, and landing-page performance.

### Should all historical data move?

Not necessarily. Legal, service, analytics, and operational needs should determine what is migrated or archived.

### Can the old store stay live during development?

Yes. Use controlled environments and plan how final data changes will be synchronized.
